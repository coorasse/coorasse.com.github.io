## One year later

It's almost a year since I wrote about [our approach to AI at Renuo](https://www.renuo.ch/blog/handmade-software) and
some things have changed since then.

I started 2026 with the firm intention of more seriously exploring AI-assisted development with agents.
Up until the end of 2025, Cursor had been my primary tool for AI-assisted development.

[New tools keep coming out at a relentless pace](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vGKC9LpGnOQ), and for someone who
actually needs to ship projects and deliver value, it's practically impossible to keep up with everything.
Unless you're a social media influencer whose only (respectable) job is to test new models and announce the end of the
world every other day, you simply cannot follow every development.

## The good news

But here's the first piece of good news: if you ignore a new AI tool for two or three months, it'll likely vanish
without a trace, which is already a great comfort and a considerable time saver.

I've been doing web development for about 20 years, and the AI tools developed between 2025 and 2026 are without a
doubt the most exciting thing to happen in all that time.

So here's my first piece of advice: if a tool has been around for more than six months, it's worth your time and worth
trying out. Everything else? Feel free to ignore it and spend that energy on something actually useful.

## Our approach

At Renuo we don't have a unified approach right now and, as is typical for us, we give a lot of freedom to each person
to choose and use the tools they prefer.
We have people with Copilot licenses, others on Claude, others on OpenAI. An accounting nightmare.
But it does allow us to explore a wide range of options simultaneously and compare results: if we weren't so spread out,
we wouldn't be able to cover such a broad landscape.

So, long live a decentralized approach! It won't stay this way forever: sooner or later, shared skills and best
practices will emerge, as they already have in many other areas of the company, but for now: maximum freedom!

Sharing happens in plenty of ways: beyond dedicated Slack channels for experiences and tips, we have a public feed
at https://til.renuo.ch, and several recurring meetings: every two months we hold evening "beer talks", and every six
weeks our all-hands, where everyone shares progress on their work.
Sharing is, in fact, the most important piece right now.

## What I'm seeing

This significant shift brings important changes, but some foundations remain: first and foremost, the quality of the
work itself.

Renuo is a company with deep roots in software engineering. Engineering is the backbone of our web agency, and there are
no shortcuts.

Every line of code produced at Renuo still goes through the four-eyes principle and doesn't reach production without
thorough review.

Sure, much of the code written at Renuo today is produced with AI assistance, but it's simply
a new, far more efficient tool for writing code. Everything else hasn't changed. And everything else matters enormously.

## Engineering at the core

Despite how much models have improved (and they'll keep improving), I can't help but notice that the quality of what
they produce is mediocre at best. 
Which is perfectly normal, and it's already a fundamental help, but it's not the final destination.

A few concrete examples:

AI helps me enormously today when exploring different options: I can visualize several approaches to the same problem in
a very short time. In minutes or hours, I can have three different approaches already implemented and working, side by
side, ready to compare. A year ago, I could only sketch this out on paper, or imagine it in purely abstract terms. Today
it's tangible. Amazing!
What usually happens next is that the quality of what's been produced is rough, so it
takes [many further iterations](https://coorasse.com/blog/iterating-with-ai/) to reach the desired result. But still...
wow.

AI today can be enormously helpful when exploring an unfamiliar project. Renuo is a web agency and we have something
like 300 repositories and at least a hundred projects. Even I, who have worked here for 15 years, don't know all of
them. Being able to ask AI to walk me through a new project is genuinely great. Nothing replaces 30 minutes with someone
who's been working on it for months or years, but at least I can get to that meeting with a basic understanding of
what the project does.

Bug identification is another area where AI can sometimes offer real help. In my experience, there's a good 50% chance
it'll find the actual root cause. It often proposes the wrong fix, but at least it helped pinpoint the problem. 50% is a
lot. A huge amount, actually.

One area where modern tools still don't help me is making the right engineering decisions. Over 20 years, I've developed
a very particular and specific taste for how I want things done, and AI still has a decidedly "generic" aesthetic. I'm
clearly trying to adapt it to my way of working, but I think the nuances are endless, and this may never be fully
achievable.

The engineering dimension is more important than ever: if up until 2024 you could get by with modest software
engineering skills as long as you were a decent coder, that's no longer the case. Thankfully.
How fast you type code is now irrelevant: you need to know how to do software architecture.
That's why I'm re-reading Clean Code, The Clean Coder, and Clean Architecture.

Being an excellent engineer is what will give you a bright future in this field. But one increasingly important
dimension will be understanding the business side of the software you're building: a blind approach to development,
without caring about the *why* behind a given feature, is what makes a person indistinguishable from an AI agent that
simply, always, does what it's told.

## AI as part of the process

Integrating AI smoothly into existing workflows is what I've been focused on in the past few weeks.
The idea is that AI agents can become part of Renuo to assist us with specific tasks, without requiring our workflow to
change around them. This is a crucial distinction: rather than building processes around AI, the goal is to make AI a
natural part of the processes we already have. This is how Renuo will get the most out of AI's potential without
compromising on the quality of our work: engineering at the core.
